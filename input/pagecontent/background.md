# Background

In the U.S., health data collection for key demographic domains is inconsistent, incomplete, and often uses ambiguous or non-inclusive concepts. This limits the ability of clinicians, health systems, payers, public health, and researchers to reliably identify disparities, measure outcomes, design culturally responsive interventions, and monitor progress toward equity. The DEMo IG addresses these gaps by specifying standardized, granular, and person-centered data elements and exchange mechanisms that preserve respectful language and improve data quality and usability.

From 2020 to 2023, AHIP convened a workgroup that developed modernized and enhanced demographic data content standards through a multi-stakeholder-driven process resulting in voluntary best practices for health plans. AHIP identified that the impact of this work would be increased by creation of accredited standards. Thus, AHIP began a collaborative effort with Civitas and HL7 with the goal of modernizing both demographic content and exchange standards so that they are person centered, culturally sensitive, sufficiently granular, and designed in a manner that allows for aligned across stakeholders.

Beginning January 2024, AHIP, Civitas, and HL7 convened a diverse group of more than 130 participants-representing health plans, federal agencies, provider organizations, consumer and patient advocates, standards bodies, and other stakeholders. Across five national virtual focus groups, participants reviewed and refined straw-man questions and response options for demographic domains including race, ethnicity, language preference, sexual orientation, sex, gender, pronouns, relationship status, military experience, disability status, and spiritual beliefs. Contributors were encouraged to apply their professional and lived experience to improve wording, response choices, and cultural appropriateness.

Following the focus groups, the revised items underwent cognitive testing in collaboration with the Patient Advocate Foundation to assess comprehension and respondent comfort; further refinements were made based on those results. Finally, after identifying a sponsoring HL7 workgroup, the updated questions and response options were circulated for additional stakeholder and public review through the HL7 community.

## Intended use and interactions

- Primary Uses: Support point-of-care clinical decision-making and person-centered care; enable population-health analytics, quality measurement, and disparity monitoring; facilitate research and public-health reporting; and inform operational and policy decisions to advance health equity.
- Collection Points and Actors: Demographic data are intended to be captured primarily via patient self-report at registration, intake, patient portals, or structured patient interviews. Clinicians, registration staff, and authorized proxies may also record or update elements when self-report is not feasible, following documented consent and verification practices.
- Systems and Exchange: Data capture occurs in EHRs and patient engagement systems and is shared with registries, analytics platforms, payer systems, public-health agencies, HIEs, and research databases. The IG defines FHIR resources, profiles, value sets, and mapping guidance to enable consistent exchange across systems, with support for persistence, updates, provenance, and consent metadata.
